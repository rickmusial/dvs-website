Founder Counterintuition

Carrying a no you went looking for

R
Rick Musial Founder & Chairman, DVS
17 August 2026
3 min read

A while ago I wrote that the honest move is to stop waiting for a yes and go looking for the no – to hunt for the reason your idea doesn’t hold up, on purpose, before the market finds it for you. I still believe that’s the discipline. What I didn’t write about, because I hadn’t yet felt it clearly enough to name, is what happens after the hunt succeeds.

You find it. The no is real, and it’s correct, and it’s yours. And then there are a few days – before you act on it, before you tell anyone, before it becomes a thing that has happened – where you just carry it.

The gap between knowing and acting

Those days are quieter than I expected. The verdict is in; the work of reaching it is done. But you don’t act the same hour you know. There’s timing, sequence, the people who need to hear it in the right order. So the no sits with you, fully formed, while the world around you carries on treating the venture as alive. You answer an email about it. Someone congratulates you on it. And you already know.

Why there’s no one to hand it to

The strange part is who you can’t take it to. The instinct is to tell the people closest to you – and those are precisely the people least able to hold it, because they have a stake in your hope. I’ve written before that encouragement is not a second opinion: the people who love you are compromised graders, not out of dishonesty but out of care. That’s a useful thing to know while you’re deciding. It’s a lonely thing once you’ve decided, because the first person you want to tell will, gently, try to talk you back toward the yes. Not because they’re wrong about you – because they can’t help it.

The verdict I could reach with help. The days after it, I carried mostly alone.

What it actually is

I want to be careful here, because it would be easy to dress this up. It isn’t relief – that comes later, if it comes. It isn’t grief, exactly. It’s closer to holding something true and heavy that you can’t yet set down, and having nowhere obvious to set it. You made the call the right way. There’s just a distance now between you and the people who were still cheering, and you’re standing in it for a while.

Here’s the only thing I’d want a founder to take from this: the loneliness of those days is not evidence that you got it wrong. It’s the cost of having gone looking for the truth instead of waiting for the market to mail it to you later at a much higher price. A no you hunted for and found, honestly, doesn’t become wrong just because it was hard to carry. Carry it. Then act. The company of people who understand it comes afterwards – and it does come.
